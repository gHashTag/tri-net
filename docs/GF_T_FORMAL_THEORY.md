# GF-T: formal theory, theorems, and an honest placement in the literature

Status: theory note. Every theorem below is stated over the **executable** definitions in
`specs/tri_gft_*.t27` and is discharged by an in-spec assertion, an exhaustive sweep, or a
proof given here. Claims that are *not* proven are labelled as such, deliberately.

The epistemic labels are used consistently:

| label | meaning |
|---|---|
| **[PROVEN]** | mathematical proof, or exhaustive verification over a finite domain |
| **[MEASURED]** | empirical result of ours, with the measurement named |
| **[MODELED]** | analytic estimate from a cost model, not a measurement |
| **[FOLKLORE]** | widely repeated, weakly sourced — cited only to be contained |
| **[OPEN]** | we do not know |

The point of the labels is that this document should survive a hostile reviewer. Section 6
collects the claims we deliberately **refuse** to make.

## 1. The object

### 1.1 Definition (GF-T value)

A GF-T number at rung `k` is a triple `(s, o, M)` with sign `s ∈ {0,1}`, exponent offset
`o`, and significand `M`. Its value is

```
val(s, o, M) = (-1)^s · (1 + M / 2^Mbits) · 2^(o - bias)
```

with `0 ≤ o < offset_max` and `0 ≤ M < 2^Mbits`. There are **no subnormals and no taper**.

> **The exponent base is 2, not φ.** This must be said plainly, because the naming invites
> the opposite reading. `specs/tri_gft_arith.t27:9-11` writes `phi^a * phi^b = phi^(a+b)`;
> that comment asserts *exponent additivity*, which holds for any base, not base φ. The
> normative statement is `specs/tri_gft_add.t27:8-9`: "GF-T16 value = `(1 + M/512) * 2^e`".
> The test comments are unambiguous (`offset 40 = 2^0`, `2^-1 = 0.5`), and the measured
> range 2⁻⁴⁰…2⁴⁰ for GF-T16 confirms it arithmetically.
>
> φ's actual role is **the ladder geometry** (§1.2) and the anchor identity φ² + φ⁻² = 3,
> which is what ties φ to radix 3. The *exponent digits* are balanced ternary.

### 1.2 Definition (the golden ladder)

The rung geometry is generated by a Fibonacci rule (`specs/tri_gft_ladder.t27:72-77`):

```
Et(k)   = fib(k+1) + 1          exponent trits
M(k)    = fib(k+1)^2            significand bits
```

and everything else is derived:

```
offset_max(Et) = 3^Et - 1       the reserved special row (inf/nan analogue)
bias(Et)       = (3^Et - 1)/2   the unity exponent, 2^0
mant_one(M)    = 2^M            the implicit-1 significand scale
sig_bits       = M + 1
```

| rung | k | Et | M | mant_one | 3^Et | offset_max | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GF-T4 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 9 | 8 | 4 |
| GF-T8 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 16 | 27 | 26 | 13 |
| GF-T16 | 3 | 4 | 9 | 512 | 81 | 80 | 40 |
| GF-T32 | 4 | 6 | 25 | 33 554 432 | 729 | 728 | **364** |

**Theorem 1 (bias is exactly half the special row). [PROVEN]**
`2·bias(Et) = offset_max(Et)` for all Et.
*Proof.* `2·(3^Et − 1)/2 = 3^Et − 1`. Since `3^Et` is odd, `3^Et − 1` is even and the
division is exact, so the ladder is exponent-symmetric about unity by construction: the
representable exponents are `e ∈ [−bias, +bias − 1]`. ∎
Asserted in-spec at `tri_gft_ladder.t27:220-224`.

**Corollary 1.1.** GF-T16 covers `e ∈ [−40, 39]`, i.e. ~2⁸⁰ of dynamic range in 16 bits,
with **uniform** relative precision across the whole range (no taper). This is the property
posits deliberately give up; see §5.2.

**Remark (the ladder is not log-linear).** `Et(4) = 6`, not 5 — GF-T32 spends six exponent
trits (`tri_gft_ladder.t27:29-30`). A "one more trit per doubling" rule is wrong, and a
stale comment at `tri_gft_ladder.t27:115` still records the pre-ratification GF-T32 bias
`121 = (3⁵−1)/2`. The normative value is **364**; the assertion at `:223` is authoritative.
*(Found by the formal audit that produced this document; see §7.)*

**Theorem 2 (GF-T1024 is absent by construction, not by omission). [PROVEN]**
`gft_pow3` is exact through Et = 14 in u32 and Et = 35 in u64. GF-T1024 needs Et = 56, so
`3^56 ≈ 5.2·10^26` exceeds u64. The spec returns 0 rather than a truncated value
(`tri_gft_ladder.t27:23-24`, asserted `:288`).
*Significance.* The ladder **fails closed**. A format that silently returned a wrapped
modulus would produce a rung whose `offset_max` is smaller than its `bias` — every value
would classify as special. Absence is the correct behaviour.

## 2. Arithmetic

### 2.1 Multiplication

With `σ_a = mant_one + m_a`, `σ_b = mant_one + m_b`, and `P = σ_a·σ_b` (an exact integer):

```
carry  = [ P ≥ 2·mant_one² ]
mant   = carry ? P/(2·mant_one) − mant_one : P/mant_one − mant_one   (round toward zero)
offset = sat( o_a + o_b + carry − bias )
sign   = s_a XOR s_b
```

**Theorem 3 (single-step renormalization). [PROVEN]**
At most one renormalization step is ever required: `carry ∈ {0,1}`.
*Proof.* `σ ∈ [mant_one, 2·mant_one)`, so `σ_a·σ_b ∈ [mant_one², 4·mant_one²)`, i.e. the
product significand lies in `[1, 4)`. One halving suffices to return it to `[1, 2)`. ∎

**Theorem 4 (the carry is load-bearing in the exponent). [PROVEN by counterexample]**
The offset-only rule `o_a + o_b − bias` is wrong exactly when the significand product
renormalizes. For GF-T16, `(o=41, M=256) × (o=41, M=256)` — i.e. 1.5·2¹ × 1.5·2¹ = 2.25·2² —
gives offset **43**, not 42.
*Verification.* `verify_gft_mul_full(41,256,41,256, 42,64, …) = false` and `… 43,64 … = true`
(`tri_gft_arith.t27:243,251`). This is a defect that a verifier checking only the exponent
cannot see, which is why `verify_gft_mul_offset` is retained but is **not** the payment gate.

### 2.2 Addition, and its most important negative property

For `o_a ≥ o_b`, `d = o_a − o_b`:

```
sb     = (d ≥ sig_bits) ? 0 : (mant_one + m_b) >> d
sum    = (mant_one + m_a) + sb
offset = (sum ≥ 2·mant_one) ? sat_hi(o_a + 1) : o_a
mant   = (sum ≥ 2·mant_one) ? (sum >> 1) − mant_one : sum − mant_one
```

**Theorem 5 (⊕ is commutative but NOT associative). [PROVEN by counterexample]**
GF-T same-sign addition ⊕ is commutative (enforced by the `_c` entry points, asserted
`tri_gft_add.t27:179-181`). It is **not** associative, and the divergence is 1 ULP wide.
*Counterexample.* For lanes `p = [(40,64), (45,0), (46,256), (40,64)]` at GF-T16:

```
balanced tree  (p0⊕p1)⊕(p2⊕p3) = (47, 9)
left fold      ((p0⊕p1)⊕p2)⊕p3 = (47, 8)
```

Both confirmed on iverilog against `gft_add.v` (`tri_gft_add.t27:124-131`,
`tests/gft_dot_reduction_order.rs`). *Cause:* round-toward-zero renormalization. ∎

**Corollary 5.1 (reduction order is part of the specification). [PROVEN]**
Because ⊕ is non-associative, a dot-product claim is only well-defined together with a
reduction order. The canonical 4-lane reduction is the fixed balanced tree
`dot4(p₀,p₁,p₂,p₃) = (p₀⊕p₁) ⊕ (p₂⊕p₃)`.

> **This is the sharpest engineering consequence in the whole design.** A verifier that
> reduces a dot claim in any other order computes a different value and would **slash an
> honest executor**. Non-associativity is not a wart here; it is a protocol-level
> normativity requirement. Systems that leave reduction order to the implementation
> (as most GPU reductions do) cannot support this kind of dispute resolution at all.

**Theorem 6 (saturation asymmetry between × and +). [PROVEN]**
Multiplication saturates on **both** ends: underflow (`sum < bias`) → offset 0, overflow
(`r ≥ offset_max`) → `offset_max`. Addition saturates on the **overflow side only**, and
only on the carry branch. *Proof.* Same-sign addition of positive magnitudes is monotone
non-decreasing in the result magnitude, so an underflow branch is unreachable. ∎

**Theorem 7 (the zero sentinel is not injective). [PROVEN — a stated hazard]**
`gft_sub` maps exact cancellation to `(offset 0, mantissa 0)`. But offset 0 is *also* a
legitimate finite value, namely `1.0 · 2^(−bias)`. Hence the encoding of "zero" collides
with the smallest normal.
*Consequence.* Zero must be carried **out of band**; no theorem about `sub` may treat
`(0,0)` as a distinguished code point. This is a genuine limitation of the current
encoding, recorded rather than hidden.

### 2.3 Exhaustive verification [PROVEN over the finite domain]

| operation | domain swept | oracle |
|---|---|---|
| multiply | 81 × 81 offsets, 512 × 512 mantissa pairs | integer oracle |
| add | 86 436 cases | integer oracle |
| subtract | **35 046 400** cases, all offset pairs 0…79 | exact i128 round-toward-zero |

The subtract oracle is `i128`, not floating point, and the reason is itself a small result:
a round-to-nearest float oracle **loses the sub-ULP operand** in near-cancellation and would
certify a wrong answer as right. (`docs/VERIFIABLE_COMPUTE.md:56-72`.)

## 3. The verification ring

### 3.1 The separation that motivates everything

**Proposition 8 (signature ⊥ correctness).**
A signature attests *who* produced a result. It says nothing about whether the result is
*correct*. A dishonest executor can sign a perfectly fresh, self-consistent receipt for a
wrong GF-T product and be paid (`tri_compute_challenge.t27:1-9`).

The architecture therefore supplies two independent mechanisms: signatures for identity,
and **recomputation** for correctness.

**Theorem 9 (single-op disputes need no bisection). [PROVEN]**
Because GF-T operations are deterministic and bit-exact, and because §2.3 pins them against
an exhaustive oracle, any challenger can settle a single-op dispute **by equality**:
recompute `gf_op(a,b)` and compare.
*Contrast.* Interactive-fraud-proof systems (Gensyn Verde, Arbitrum-style rollups) need
logarithmic-round bisection because their computations are long and not bit-reproducible.
Here the dispute is one comparison. The cost of a fraud proof is O(1), not O(log n).

*The precondition is doing real work:* bit-exactness must hold **across backends**. Which is
why the corpus is executed under four independent backends and drift-checked byte-for-byte
(§7).

### 3.2 The anchor, and why bare equality is unsound

**Theorem 10 (bare equality is exploitable in both directions). [PROVEN — it was the bug]**
`resolve(claimed, recomputed)` trusts the caller to have recomputed on the operands the
executor actually committed. Without an anchor:
1. a lying **challenger** recomputes on operands the executor never signed → false slash;
2. a lying **executor** answers with operands it never committed → escaped slash.

The fix is the receipt **leaf anchor** binding `(fmt, width, op, a, b, result, executor,
epoch)` (`tri_compute_challenge.t27:62-74`).

**Theorem 11 (anchor width sets the forgery bound). [PROVEN]**
A 32-bit leaf gives a birthday bound of ~2¹⁶ — inadequate against a resourced adversary.
The `_d256` resolvers anchor on the full SHA-256 digest, giving ~2¹²⁸ (`:302-308`).

### 3.3 Guard ordering is normative

The resolver chain is **freshness → family → rung → leaf anchor → ternary → bit-exact
honesty**, and the order is not cosmetic.

**Theorem 12 (freshness must precede everything, and must not advance on non-terminal
outcomes). [PROVEN]**
Replay protection is strict (`dispute_epoch > last_epoch`). The watermark advances **only**
on terminal HONEST/SLASH. *Proof of necessity.* If a non-terminal outcome advanced the
watermark, an adversary submits one malformed dispute at a high epoch, the watermark jumps,
and every honest future dispute is rejected as stale — a replay-nonce DoS
(`tri_compute_challenge.t27:136-142`). ∎

**Theorem 13 (the hardened resolvers are conservative extensions). [PROVEN]**
Each layer (`_bound`, `_full`, `_d256`, `_bitnet`, `_quorum`) adds rejections and never
suppresses a genuine slash. Verified by `fresh_fraud_still_slashes`: a fresh, same-family,
leaf-matching, mismatched-result dispute still yields SLASH through every wrapper.
*Significance.* Monotonicity in the rejection direction is what makes the stack safely
composable; it is asserted, not assumed.

**Theorem 14 (rung must be bound to any finiteness claim). [PROVEN]**
Offset 80 is the special row at GF-T16 and an ordinary finite value at GF-T32. Therefore
`is_finite` is **not** a property of an offset; it is a property of `(offset, Et)`. A
challenger recomputing at the wrong rung compares wrong numbers and must not slash — hence
`RESOLVE_RUNG_MISMATCH` (`tri_compute_challenge.t27:189-198`).

### 3.4 The payment gate

**Theorem 15 (payability ≡ validity, quantified). [PROVEN over the tested domain]**
For all `o ∈ {0, 1, 40, 79, 80, 81, 100, 1000, u32::MAX}`:

```
payable_flag(FMT_GFT, o, ·,·,·, 80) = 1   ⟺   is_valid_gft(o, 4)
```

where `is_valid_gft(o,Et) ⟺ o < 3^Et − 1`.
*Why it is stated as an agreement theorem rather than two definitions.* The old gate tested
`== offset_max`, which caught the special row but **classified an out-of-range offset as
payable** — a crafted `gf_result` past the ladder was paid. Range-blindness was a
garbage-pay hole (`tri_compute_settle.t27:107-110`). Pinning the two functions to each other
is what stops them drifting apart again.

**Theorem 16 (the mint gate is a strict 4-way conjunction). [PROVEN]**
`payable_authentic = sig_ok ∧ ¬already_settled ∧ fresh ∧ finite`, and each conjunct is
independently necessary (all four single-guard drops verified). A valid signature never
bypasses freshness, finiteness, or settled-once.

### 3.5 Economics

**Theorem 17 (no value is created). [PROVEN]**
`total3 = balance + locked + pending` is conserved across every account operation, with
"no fourth bucket" (`tri_compute_account.t27:79-81, 164-170`). The pool path preserves
`total(balance, pool)` across a settle.

**Theorem 18 (every credit path saturates; no slash is ever a credit). [PROVEN]**
For all `b, x`: `bal_add_sat(b,x) ≥ b`, and it never wraps down. And
`outcome ≠ HONEST ⇒ balance_after_resolve = balance`.
*The concrete near-miss.* `burned_total5(…, stake=1.1e9)` must be 4·1.1e9 = 4.4e9, which
exceeds u32. A bare multiply wraps to **105 032 704**; the saturating version pins
`u32::MAX`. Both values are in the test, so the regression cannot silently return.

**Theorem 19 (the reputation cap must be computed in u64). [PROVEN by counterexample]**
`rep_after_honest(rep, gain) = min(rep + gain, REP_MAX)` computed in u32 wraps for
`gain = 4 294 966 296`, giving sum 0, which passes the `> REP_MAX` guard — so **honest work
would zero a maxed reputation**. Widening to u64 makes the cap total over all u32 gains.

**Theorem 20 (verification is self-funding). [PROVEN]**
Burned dissenter stake funds honest-verifier reward by floor division:
`honest_count · ⌊burned/honest_count⌋ ≤ burned`. Hence the scheme never over-issues; dust
stays unminted. This is the verifier's-dilemma fix — verification is net-positive **without
protocol inflation**.

**Theorem 21 (griefing outcomes burn; non-fault outcomes do not). [PROVEN]**
The 6-outcome set partitions into challenger-fault (HONEST, MALFORMED, FAMILY_MISMATCH →
stake burned) and non-fault (SLASH, STALE, INDETERMINATE → stake kept). The subtle case is
**INDETERMINATE**: a split verifier set proves nothing, and burning the challenger's stake
there would penalize a legitimate challenge for verifier behaviour, chilling challenges
exactly when a quorum is uncertain.

**Theorem 22 (collateral must scale with exposure). [PROVEN — a stated attack]**
A fixed bond is toothless once a node handles more value than the bond: a fraudster with
bond = 1 across 1000 tasks loses ~nothing. Hence
`required_bond = ⌊outstanding · min_bps / 10000⌋`, enforced at ingress.
Coupled to rung risk: `rung_min_bps = min_bps + max(0, Et−4)·500` (+5% per exponent trit
above GF-T16) and `window_for_rung(Et) = 64 + max(0, Et−4)·16` epochs — higher rung ⇒
bigger bond **and** longer challenge window.

**Theorem 23 (identity binding requires a hash preimage, not a name). [PROVEN]**
The assignee id is public, so binding a result to an assignment is forgeable on its own. It
becomes unforgeable only combined with `executor_id = low32(SHA-256(pubkey))`: *a forger can
name the assignee but cannot also produce a signature whose key hashes to it*
(`tri_a2a.t27:371-380`).

**Theorem 24 ((family, op) is a lossy skill proxy — the precision-downgrade attack).
[PROVEN]**
`skill_op` and `skill_family` are **equal** for `SKILL_GFT16_MUL` and `SKILL_GFT8_MUL`; only
`skill_width` distinguishes them. Therefore the width-blind gate binds a **GF-T8** receipt to
a **GF-T16** assignment — an executor is paid GF-T16 rates for 8-bit work. The fix is a
strictly-additional conjunct: `admit_result_signed_sized = width_match ∧ admit_result_signed`.
`{family, op, width}` identifies a hosted skill; `{family, op}` does not.

**Theorem 25 (the 2-bit ternary packing is non-injective). [PROVEN]**
BitNet-style packing decodes `0b00` and `0b11` **both** to weight 0. A test `trit != 0`
counts `0b11` as active and **over-reports attested work** — and the receipt attests
*untrusted* weights, so a malicious packing inflates the active-weight count. Correct
predicate: `is_active(t) ⟺ t ∈ {1,2}`.

## 4. What the ternary claim rests on — and what it does not

This section exists because the surrounding literature is full of numbers that do not mean
what they are quoted to mean.

**Proposition 26 (radix economy). [PROVEN, and narrower than usually cited]**
For the cost functional `E(b,N) = b·ln N / ln b`, minimizing `b/ln b` over real `b > 1` gives
`b = e` (Steiner, *Crelle's Journal* **40**, 1850). Over integers:

```
2/ln2 ≈ 2.885390
3/ln3 ≈ 2.730718   ← minimum
4/ln4 ≈ 2.885390   ← EXACTLY equal to base 2, since 4/ln4 = 4/(2ln2) = 2/ln2
```

So the correct statement is: **3 minimizes `b/ln b` over the integers, and 2 and 4 tie
exactly for second.** The popular phrasing "3 wins because it is closest to e" is
coincidence-shaped reasoning — 4 is *farther* from e than 2 and yet ties it exactly.

**Proposition 27 (the cost model's own authors disclaimed it). [DOCUMENTED]**
The canonical computing source for radix economy is ERA, *High-Speed Computing Devices*
(McGraw-Hill, 1950). It says radix 3 is most economical — and then, in the next sentence:

> "These assumptions are, of course, only approximately valid, and the choice of 2 as a
> radix is frequently justified on more complete analysis."

Any document citing radix economy without this disclaimer is citing selectively. **We cite
it.** The cost premise ("cost ∝ b per digit") describes vacuum-tube ring counters; it is
empirically false for CMOS, where intermediate voltage levels cost static power, noise
margin, and threshold engineering.

**Proposition 28 (radix economy has never decided a real design). [DOCUMENTED]**
The IBM 650 — a vacuum-tube machine, exactly the technology the 1950 model describes — chose
bi-quinary. Every transistor-era machine chose binary. Setun's ~50 units were built and
production stopped in 1965.

**Remark on Setun. [FOLKLORE — self-reported]**
The oft-quoted "the binary replacement cost 2.5× as much and performed no better" and "one
seventh as many elements" come from Brusentsov, the designer, in retrospective accounts.
There is no independent contemporaneous benchmark. Cite as "Brusentsov reports", never as
measurement. (Brusentsov & Ramil Alvarez, SoRuCom 2006, IFIP AICT 357, pp. 74–80.)

**Remark on Knuth. [AESTHETIC, not a theorem]**
"Perhaps the prettiest number system of all is the balanced ternary notation" (*TAOCP* Vol 2,
§4.1, p. 190) is Knuth's judgment. What *is* proven on those pages: negation is a digit-wise
sign flip (no sign bit, no ±0 asymmetry), rounding to nearest is truncation, and the digit
ratio vs binary is `ln2/ln3 ≈ 0.6309`. Knuth's remark that ternary circuit complexity "is not
much greater" carries **no cost model** — it is not a hardware result.

**Attribution correctness.** Balanced ternary is **not** due to Knuth or Setun: the weights
problem is in Bachet (1624), and the first explicit description of the notation is Lalanne,
*Comptes Rendus*, 1840.

**Proposition 29 (φ-representation: what is actually proven).**
For φ = (1+√5)/2 with φ² = φ + 1 (Bergman, *Mathematics Magazine* **31**(2), 1957, 98–110 —
written at age 12):
- **[PROVEN]** the carry identity `φⁿ = φⁿ⁻¹ + φⁿ⁻²`, i.e. `011_φ = 100_φ`;
- **[PROVEN]** every non-negative integer has a **unique terminating** standard-form
  expansion (digits {0,1}, no "11" substring);
- **[PROVEN]** finite representations are exactly the elements of `ℤ[φ]`;
- **[FALSE as often stated]** "φ-representations are unique" — uniqueness holds **only**
  relative to the standard form. `1_φ = 0.11_φ`, exactly analogous to 0.999… = 1.
- **[PROVEN]** `φⁿ = Fₙφ + Fₙ₋₁`, so the no-"11" constraint and Zeckendorf's
  no-consecutive-Fibonacci constraint are the *same* combinatorial condition. Base-φ standard
  form restricted to integers **is** Zeckendorf. These are not independent facts.
- Attribution: the theorem is commonly credited to Zeckendorf (1972) but was **first
  published by Lekkerkerker (1952)**, generalizing Ostrowski (1922).

**Prior art we must cite and distinguish.** A. Stakhov, "Brousentsov's Ternary Principle,
Bergman's Number System and Ternary Mirror-symmetrical Arithmetic," *The Computer Journal*
**45**(2), 2002, 221–236 — an explicit balanced-ternary × φ-base synthesis. The arithmetic
constructions there are checkable and legitimately published; the surrounding "Mathematics of
Harmony" programme is not a mathematical support and we do not lean on it.

**Where GF-T actually sits.** GF-T is **not** a φ-radix format. Its exponent is base 2 with
balanced-ternary *digits*; φ enters only as the ladder-geometry generator. So Proposition 29
is background, not foundation — and saying otherwise would be the single easiest way to lose
a reviewer.

## 5. Placement against contemporary formats

### 5.1 The comparison table

| format | exponent | taper | relative precision | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IEEE-754 binary16 | 5 bits | no | uniform | subnormals |
| bfloat16 | 8 bits | no | uniform, coarse | FP32 range; **no defining paper** (Google Brain / TPUv2) |
| FP8 E4M3 | 4 bits | no | uniform | **not IEEE-conformant** — drops infinities, one NaN, to buy a mantissa bit |
| posit16 | regime + es=2 | **yes** | non-uniform | es fixed at 2 by the 2022 standard, contradicting the 2017 paper |
| MX (block) | shared E8M0 per 32 | per-block | uniform in block | OCP spec is the format authority; arXiv:2310.10537 is the evaluation |
| **GF-T16** | 4 balanced-ternary trits | **no** | **uniform** | ~2⁸⁰ range, no subnormals |

### 5.2 The posit contrast is the substantive one

**Proposition 30 (tapering costs scale invariance of accuracy). [PUBLISHED RESULT]**
de Dinechin, Forget, Muller & Uguen, "Posits: the good, the bad and the ugly" (CoNGA 2019):
under IEEE-754 the relative error is near-uniform across the dynamic range; under posits it
is not. Consequently an algorithm's error behaviour becomes dependent on the **magnitude** of
its data, so numerical analyses do not transfer. The authors call unconditional replacement
of floats by posits *"extremely dangerous"*, and recommend posits as a **storage** format
with computation in a wider one.

**This is the axis on which GF-T differs, and we should argue on it rather than on radix
mysticism.** GF-T is deliberately **untapered**: uniform relative precision, so error
analyses transfer. The price is that GF-T does not buy extra accuracy near |x| ≈ 1. That is a
trade, stated as a trade.

### 5.3 The ternary-NN energy argument — what survives scrutiny

- **[MEASURED, Horowitz ISSCC 2014]** at 45 nm: FP32 MAC 4.6 pJ, FP32 add 0.9 pJ, INT8 MAC
  0.2 pJ, INT8 add 0.03 pJ. Integer add is ~an order of magnitude cheaper than multiply; both
  are dwarfed by DRAM (~100× a MAC). **This is the solid foundation.**
- **[PROVEN, trivially]** multiplication by an element of {−1,0,+1} needs no multiplier — it
  is negate/zero/pass, then accumulate.
- **[MEASURED, single lab]** BitNet b1.58 (Ma et al., arXiv:2402.17764) at 3B params matches
  FP16 LLaMA perplexity with 3.55× less memory and 2.71× lower latency.
- **[MARKETING]** "1-bit LLM" is a misnomer by the paper's own body; log₂3 = 1.585 is an
  information bound, while deployments store 2 b/weight or pack 5 trits in 8 bits (3⁵=243)
  for ~1.6 b/weight. And **activations are 8-bit**: the matmul is ternary × INT8, so the
  multiplier elimination is *weight-side*.
- **[MODELED, not measured]** the 71.4× energy figure is arithmetic-only, obtained by
  substituting Horowitz constants into an operation count, and **excludes memory movement** —
  which dominates real LLM inference energy.
- **[PUBLISHED COUNTER-RESULT]** Malekar, Elbtity & Zand, "Matmul or No Matmul in the Era of
  1-bit LLMs" (arXiv:2408.11939): ternary quantization is applied to projection layers only,
  attention stays higher precision, and an **Amdahl bound** limits end-to-end speedup. Their
  stated motivation is to avoid "fundamentally wrong choices of goals in future research".
- **[OPEN]** the residual accuracy gap. Liu & Liu, "Ternary Quantization: A Survey"
  (arXiv:2303.01505) identifies straight-through-estimator gradient bias as the recurring
  root cause — narrowed by method-level fixes, not closed.
- **[NOT ESTABLISHED]** that ternary hardware is more efficient than binary **at parity**. No
  fabricated ternary accelerator has published measured end-to-end joules-per-token at
  accuracy parity with an FP16 baseline.

---

## 6. Claims we refuse to make

Stated explicitly, because a theory document is judged as much by what it declines to assert:

1. **We do not claim ternary hardware is more efficient than binary.** No measured,
   fabricated, at-parity result exists — ours or anyone's.
2. **We do not claim radix 3 is optimal for hardware.** The theorem is about a cost
   functional whose premise fails for CMOS, and its own 1950 source disclaims it.
3. **We do not claim GF-T is a φ-radix format.** The exponent base is 2.
4. **We do not claim GF-T beats IEEE-754 universally.** It is untapered and range-symmetric;
   that is a trade, and near |x| ≈ 1 a tapered format has more precision to offer.
5. **We do not present the ×3 / ×5.5 comparison figures as independent.** They are our own
   measurements against one competitor implementation, on our own harness — reproducible, but
   not third-party.
6. **We do not claim the exhaustive sweeps prove correctness of the *design*.** They prove
   the implementation matches the oracle on a finite domain. Whether the design is the right
   one is a separate question.

---

## 7. Method: why these theorems are checkable at all

Every theorem above is discharged against **executable** artifacts, and the infrastructure
that makes that trustworthy is itself the subject of a long hardening effort:

- the corpus (107 specs) is executed under **Icarus Verilog** (hardware simulation), **C**,
  and **Zig**, and byte-drift-checked against **Rust**, with `gen/` as the single source of
  truth so no generated artifact can escape its gate;
- coverage floors on every leg make a "gate that checks nothing" fail loudly;
- the gates were **negatively audited** — each was broken on purpose to prove it can redden.
  That audit found the required Icarus gate had been **blind to every test failure** (a
  pipeline's exit status is `tee`'s without `pipefail`), 18 compute-ring invariants that were
  never executed (an undeclared cargo test target), and 28 of 29 binaries never compiled
  (`autobins = false`).

The last point is the methodological one worth generalizing:

> **A green check is worth nothing until you have proven the check can go red.**

The stale GF-T32 bias comment noted in §1.2 was found the same way — by re-deriving the
ladder from `fib` rather than trusting the prose.
